## Evidence map

| Classification                 | Verified statement permitted in the reel                                                                                                                               | Boundary                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Strong scientific evidence     | Implementation intentions are plans in an “if situation Y, then I will do action Z” format that specify when, where, or how to act toward an already chosen goal.      | A plan supports an existing goal; it does not create commitment, resources, or skill by itself.                                                            |
| Strong scientific evidence     | A 2006 meta-analysis of 94 independent tests reported a medium-to-large average association of implementation-intention formation with goal attainment (d = .65).      | This average does not mean every person, goal, or setting receives the same benefit.                                                                       |
| Mechanism supported by reviews | Reviews describe two proposed component processes: the planned cue can become easier to notice, and the cue can become more strongly linked to the planned response.   | “Automatic” here is a research description of cue-linked action initiation, not proof that a person has no control or that the brain works like a machine. |
| Direct limitation evidence     | In a randomized field experiment with 877 gym members, a simple prompt to choose future workout days and times did not increase average gym attendance over two weeks. | Do not present if–then planning as a guaranteed solution for repeated behavior; effects are context-dependent.                                             |
| Expert/review interpretation   | A concrete cue can reduce the need to decide from scratch at the critical moment.                                                                                      | This is an interpretation of the proposed cue–response mechanism, not a promise of motivation or a clinical intervention.                                  |
| Not used                       | Treatment, diagnosis, medication adherence, substance-use, exercise, diet, trauma, or mental-health advice.                                                            | This reel is general behavioral-science education, not health or therapeutic guidance.                                                                     |
| Philosophy/spirituality        | No philosophical or spiritual claim is used.                                                                                                                           | None should be presented as behavioral science.                                                                                                            |
