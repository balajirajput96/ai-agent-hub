## Evidence map

| Classification                | Verified statement permitted in the reel                                                                                                                                                                | Boundary                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Established research question | Choice-overload research examines whether large option sets can sometimes reduce motivation to choose, confidence, satisfaction, or increase deferral and regret.                                       | “More options” does not automatically cause overload in every setting.                                                      |
| Mixed meta-analytic evidence  | One meta-analysis of 63 conditions found a mean effect near zero but substantial variation between studies.                                                                                             | Do not present a universal “six options is best” rule or a fixed limit for the human mind.                                  |
| Moderated evidence            | A later meta-analysis of 99 observations identified decision-task difficulty, choice-set complexity, preference uncertainty, and effort-minimizing goals as moderators associated with choice overload. | These are research-level moderators; they do not diagnose an individual or determine whether any particular choice is good. |
| Review interpretation         | Choice can also have benefits, including better matching of diverse preferences; whether a large set helps or hinders depends on context and the decision-maker.                                        | Avoid treating limited choice as inherently superior or using the effect to pressure people into a decision.                |
| Safe expert interpretation    | For a low-stakes personal choice, grouping comparable options or first deciding the most relevant attributes can make the comparison process clearer.                                                   | This is an optional educational reflection prompt, not financial, medical, legal, consumer, or mental-health advice.        |
| Excluded claims               | “Decision fatigue,” cognitive-capacity numbers, guaranteed satisfaction, shopping/investment recommendations, or claims that a person is indecisive because of a disorder.                              | Excluded because the cited evidence does not establish those individual conclusions.                                        |
| Philosophy/spirituality       | No philosophical or spiritual claim is used.                                                                                                                                                            | None should be represented as empirical evidence.                                                                           |

## Sources

1. Scheibehenne B, Greifeneder R, Todd PM. _Can There Ever Be Too Many Options? A Meta-Analytic Review of Choice Overload._ Journal of Consumer Research, 2010;37(3):409–425. The meta-analysis reported a mean effect virtually equal to zero with considerable variation across studies, and did not identify sufficient conditions. [Abstract and article record](https://doi.org/10.1086/651235).
2. Chernev A, Böckenholt U, Goodman J. _Choice overload: A conceptual review and meta-analysis._ Journal of Consumer Psychology, 2015;25(2):333–358. Across 99 observations, the review identifies choice-set complexity, task difficulty, preference uncertainty, and decision goal as moderators. [Abstract and article record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2014.08.002).
3. Misuraca R, Nixon AE, Miceli S, Di Stefano G, Scaffidi Abbate C. _On the advantages and disadvantages of choice: future research directions in choice overload and its moderators._ Frontiers in Psychology, 2024;15:1290359. The review discusses both benefits and disadvantages of choice, and emphasizes contextual and individual moderators. [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11111947/).
