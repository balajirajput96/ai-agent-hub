## Evidence map

| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Emerging evidence       | In controlled studies using aversive images, selecting words for feelings was associated with lower self-reported distress than passive viewing in some conditions. | This does not establish a universal benefit in daily life, nor a substitute for professional support.                     |
| Emerging evidence       | A 2022 experimental study found that effects varied with the intensity of the stimulus; its authors explicitly advise caution in low-intensity conditions.          | The finding comes from a specific laboratory task and should not be turned into crisis or mental-health treatment advice. |

## Sources

1. Lieberman MD, Inagaki TK, Tabibnia G, Crockett MJ. _Subjective Responses to Emotional Stimuli During Labeling, Reappraisal, and Distraction._ Emotion, 2011. Four controlled studies reported lower self-reported distress during affect labeling than passive viewing for negative images, with boundaries by intensity and measure. [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3444304/).
2. Levy-Gigi E, Shamay-Tsoory S. _Affect labeling: The role of timing and intensity._ PLOS ONE, 2022. Controlled experiments found effects depended on stimulus intensity; the authors caution against standard general advice in low-intensity conditions. [Open-access article](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0279303).
3. Kircanski K, Lieberman MD, Craske MG. _Feelings Into Words: Contributions of Language to Exposure Therapy._ Psychological Science, 2012. Clinical-context research offers a narrower, mixed-outcome translation example; it is not used as personal treatment guidance. [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4721564/).
