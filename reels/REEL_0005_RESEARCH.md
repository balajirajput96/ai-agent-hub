## Evidence map

| Classification        | Verified statement permitted in the reel                                                                                                                                            | Boundary                                                                                                         |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Strong evidence       | Reviews of learning research identify retrieval practice and spacing as strategies that can enhance learning across many educational settings.                                      | Effects depend on task, feedback, learner, delay, and implementation; do not promise marks or universal results. |
| Strong evidence       | In a 2021 systematic review of 50 classroom experiments, most reported effect sizes showed medium or large retrieval-practice benefits.                                             | The review notes limited evidence from non-WEIRD settings; the statistic is context, not a personal prediction.  |
| Emerging evidence     | A 2025 primary-school study found its testing procedure outperformed simple rereading for the materials and conditions studied, while its spacing manipulation was not significant. | Do not claim every quiz, every interval, or every subject works identically.                                     |
| Expert interpretation | Retrieval practice means attempting to recall learned information, for example with a low-stakes question, then checking feedback.                                                  | It is not a claim that testing is the only effective study method.                                               |
| Not used              | “Stop rereading completely” or “active recall guarantees top marks.”                                                                                                                | Excluded as overgeneralized or unsupported.                                                                      |

## Sources

1. Carpenter SK, Pan SC, Butler AC. _The science of effective learning with spacing and retrieval practice._ Nature Reviews Psychology, 2022. Review of retrieval practice, spacing, metacognition, and educational applications. [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-022-00089-1).
2. Agarwal PK, Nunes LD, Blunt JR. _Retrieval Practice Consistently Benefits Student Learning: a Systematic Review of Applied Research in Schools and Classrooms._ Educational Psychology Review, 2021. The review coded 50 classroom experiments and reports both benefits and generalizability boundaries. [Article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9).
3. Franzoi L, Cembrani V, Mulatti C, Treccani B. _Retrieval practice enhances learning in real primary school settings, whether distributed or not._ Frontiers in Psychology, 2025. The study supports testing over rereading in its school setting and reports a non-significant spacing manipulation. [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12372469/).
