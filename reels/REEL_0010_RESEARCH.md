## Evidence map

| Classification                | Verified statement permitted in the reel                                                                                                                                                       | Boundary                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Established definition        | Cognitive reappraisal refers to reinterpreting the meaning of an emotion-eliciting situation or stimulus in an effort to change its emotional impact.                                          | It is a description of a strategy, not proof that a person can choose or erase every emotion.                                                        |
| Strong neuroimaging synthesis | A meta-analysis of human neuroimaging studies identified recurring patterns during instructed reappraisal tasks, including regions linked to cognitive control and meaning-related processing. | These group-level task associations do not diagnose an individual, prove that one region causes emotion, or establish a guaranteed everyday outcome. |
| Context-dependent evidence    | Reviews comparing reappraisal and acceptance emphasize that strategy effects can depend on the emotional situation and the person’s available resources.                                       | Do not claim that reframing is always superior, that people should “think positive,” or that difficult events are merely interpretation errors.      |
| Expert/review interpretation  | Asking “Is there another evidence-consistent way to interpret this situation?” can illustrate the strategy without requiring a forced positive answer.                                         | This is an educational example, not mental-health treatment, crisis advice, trauma processing, or a substitute for professional support.             |
| Excluded claims               | Cure, symptom reduction, brain rewiring, guaranteed calm, trauma recovery, relationship advice, and diagnostic claims.                                                                         | The cited literature does not justify these individual or clinical promises.                                                                         |
| Philosophy/spirituality       | No philosophical or spiritual claim is used.                                                                                                                                                   | None should be presented as scientific evidence.                                                                                                     |

This meta-analysis synthesizes instructed reappraisal neuroimaging studies; it supports cautious group-level mechanism language rather than individual prediction.
