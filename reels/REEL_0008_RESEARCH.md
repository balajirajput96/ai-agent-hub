## Evidence map

| Classification                 | Verified statement permitted in the reel                                                                                                                      | Boundary                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Established laboratory finding | In task-switching experiments, people are often slower and/or less accurate on a trial where the task changes than on a task-repeat trial.                    | This is an experimental comparison, not a claim that every change of activity harms performance equally. |
| Established laboratory finding | Preparation time can reduce a switch cost in some designs, but does not necessarily eliminate it.                                                             | Do not promise that a particular break, app, or routine will eliminate switching costs.                  |
| Mixed/mechanistic evidence     | Research discusses several contributors, including task-set preparation, carry-over/interference from the previous task, cue processing, and feature binding. | Do not depict a single settled neural mechanism or say that the brain can do only one thing.             |
| Expert/review interpretation   | Task-switching paradigms are a narrow way to study cognitive control and flexibility.                                                                         | They do not diagnose attention disorders, intelligence, burnout, or productivity in a particular person. |
| Not used                       | Driving, treatment, medication, mental-health, or quantified personal productivity claims.                                                                    | Excluded because a laboratory task is not a personal safety or health assessment.                        |
| Philosophy/spirituality        | No philosophical or spiritual claim is used.                                                                                                                  | None should be presented as evidence from cognitive-control research.                                    |

## Sources

1. Schmitz F, Krämer RJ. _Task Switching: On the Relation of Cognitive Flexibility with Cognitive Capacity._ Journal of Intelligence, 2023;11(4):68. The open-access article describes task-switch paradigms, local switch costs, preparation accounts, task-set inertia, and multiple-component interpretations. [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10140903/) and [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence11040068).
2. Benini E, Koch I, Philipp AM. _Repetition costs in task switching are not equal to cue switching costs: evidence from a cue-independent context._ Psychological Research, 2024;88:910–920. The open-access study explains robust task-repetition benefits relative to switches while separating cue-related and binding explanations. [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10965575/) and [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-023-01904-x).
3. American Psychological Association. _Multitasking: Switching costs._ The public research summary describes laboratory task-switching comparisons and notes that preparation can reduce rather than necessarily remove costs. [APA page](https://www.apa.org/topics/research/multitasking).
