## Evidence map

| Classification                 | Verified statement permitted in the reel                                                                                                                                                                                | Boundary                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Established laboratory finding | Repeated exposure to initially neutral stimuli can, in many experimental settings, be accompanied by higher reported liking or preference.                                                                              | “Can” is required; the effect is not a rule that all repeated messages, people, products, or ideas become liked.           |
| Boundary evidence              | Initial stimulus valence and context can matter. In one repeated-odor experiment, neutral or mildly pleasant odors increased in pleasantness, whereas unpleasant and very pleasant odors did not show the same pattern. | This odor study does not establish the same size or pattern for every real-world category.                                 |
| Mixed/mechanistic evidence     | Accounts discuss familiarity, perceptual fluency, habituation, conditioning, and valuation processes; the psychological and neural basis is not a single settled mechanism.                                             | Do not say that a particular brain region “causes” every familiarity preference or that dopamine explains the full effect. |
| Expert/review interpretation   | The effect is most cleanly studied through controlled comparisons of differently repeated stimuli.                                                                                                                      | It is not a personal diagnosis, a guarantee of persuasion, a marketing promise, or consent.                                |
| Excluded claims                | Advertising effectiveness, interpersonal attraction, political attitude change, clinical treatment, and subliminal-persuasion claims.                                                                                   | Excluded because the cited studies do not justify universal causal claims in these settings.                               |
| Philosophy/spirituality        | No philosophical or spiritual claim is used.                                                                                                                                                                            | None should be presented as laboratory evidence.                                                                           |
